How to Do Keyword Research for Small Business SEO (Step-by-Step Guide)

Master the fundamentals of keyword research and discover the exact phrases your customers are searching for

By the RankActions teamAugust 5, 20268 min read
In this guide
  • A step-by-step research process you can run without expensive tools
  • How to judge which keywords you can realistically win
  • Turning your keyword list into pages and content that rank

If you want to attract more customers through search engines, understanding how to do keyword research is the essential first step. Keyword research forms the foundation of every successful SEO strategy, helping you understand what your potential customers are actually typing into Google, and how you can create content that meets their needs.

Many small business owners skip this crucial step and wonder why their website isn't bringing in traffic. The truth is, without proper keyword research, you're essentially creating content in the dark, hoping it reaches the right audience. This comprehensive guide will walk you through the entire process, from understanding search intent to finding profitable keyword opportunities you can actually rank for.

What Is Keyword Research and Why Does It Matter?

Keyword research is the process of identifying and analyzing the specific search terms that people use when looking for products, services, or information related to your business. It reveals not just what people are searching for, but how they're searching, how often, and how competitive those terms are.

For small businesses, effective keyword research means the difference between showing up on page one of Google or being buried on page ten where no one will find you. It helps you:

  • Understand your audience: Discover the exact language and phrases your customers use when they need what you offer
  • Prioritize your efforts: Focus on creating content around terms that will actually drive traffic and conversions
  • Find low-competition opportunities: Identify keywords where you can realistically compete, even against larger competitors
  • Build a content strategy: Create a roadmap for blog posts, landing pages, and service pages that align with customer needs

Without keyword research, you're guessing. With it, you're making informed decisions based on real data about what your customers want.

Step 1: Brainstorm Seed Keywords for Your Business

Before you dive into any tools, start with what you already know. Seed keywords are the basic, foundational terms that describe your business, products, or services. These will serve as the starting point for expanding into more specific keyword opportunities.

Think about how you would describe what you do in simple terms. If you run a bakery in Portland, your seed keywords might include:

  • bakery
  • custom cakes
  • wedding cakes
  • artisan bread
  • birthday cakes

Don't overthink this step. Write down 10-20 basic terms that relate to your business. These don't need to be the keywords you'll ultimately target—they're just the foundation for your research.

Pro Tip: Ask your sales team or customer service representatives what questions customers ask most frequently. These real-world conversations often reveal keyword opportunities you wouldn't have thought of on your own.

Step 2: How to Do Keyword Research Using Free and Paid Tools

Once you have your seed keywords, it's time to expand your list using keyword research tools. When you learn how to do keyword research effectively, you'll use a combination of tools to uncover hundreds or even thousands of potential keywords.

Free Keyword Research Tools

For small businesses just getting started, these free tools provide valuable insights:

  • Google Keyword Planner: Built into Google Ads, this tool shows search volume ranges and suggests related keywords based on your seed terms
  • Google Search Console: Shows you which keywords are already bringing people to your site, revealing opportunities to optimize existing content
  • Google Autocomplete: Simply start typing your seed keyword into Google and note the autocomplete suggestions—these are real searches people are making
  • Answer the Public: Visualizes questions and phrases people search for around your topic

Paid Keyword Research Tools

If you're serious about SEO strategy and content planning, investing in a paid tool provides deeper insights:

  • Ahrefs: Offers keyword difficulty scores, click-through rate data, and competitive analysis
  • SEMrush: Provides keyword magic tool with extensive filtering options and competitor keyword gaps
  • Moz Keyword Explorer: Shows priority scores combining volume, difficulty, and opportunity

The key is to use these tools to expand your initial seed list into a comprehensive database of potential keywords, complete with metrics like search volume, difficulty, and related terms.

Step 3: Analyze Search Intent Behind Keywords

Understanding search intent is perhaps the most critical aspect of modern keyword research. Search intent refers to the goal or purpose behind a search query—what the person is actually trying to accomplish.

There are four main types of search intent:

  • Informational: The searcher wants to learn something (e.g., "how to frost a cake")
  • Navigational: The searcher is trying to reach a specific website (e.g., "facebook login")
  • Commercial: The searcher is researching before making a purchase (e.g., "best stand mixer for baking")
  • Transactional: The searcher is ready to buy or take action (e.g., "order birthday cake online")

When you do keyword research, don't just look at search volume. Ask yourself: What is this person trying to accomplish? Can my business help them achieve that goal? Does the intent match the type of content I want to create?

For example, if you're a bakery, targeting "how to make bread" (informational intent) means creating a detailed tutorial. But targeting "fresh bread delivery" (transactional intent) means creating a service page with ordering information. Same business, completely different content needs.

Step 4: Evaluate Keyword Difficulty and Competition

Not all keywords are created equal. Some are incredibly difficult to rank for, while others present genuine opportunities for small businesses. When you're learning how to do keyword research, understanding difficulty is crucial for setting realistic expectations.

Most keyword tools provide a difficulty score, typically on a scale of 0-100. Generally speaking:

  • 0-20: Easy to rank for, often very specific long-tail keywords
  • 21-40: Moderate difficulty, achievable with quality content and some backlinks
  • 41-60: Challenging, requires strong content and established authority
  • 61-100: Very difficult, dominated by major brands and authority sites

As a small business, you'll typically want to focus on keywords in the 0-40 range, especially when you're just starting out. These give you the best chance of ranking within a reasonable timeframe.

Beyond the difficulty score, manually review the search results. Ask yourself:

  • Who's currently ranking—are they all major brands or are there smaller businesses?
  • What type of content is ranking—blog posts, product pages, videos?
  • How comprehensive and high-quality is the existing content?
  • Can you create something genuinely better or more helpful?
Important: Search volume isn't everything. A keyword with 100 searches per month and low competition is often more valuable than one with 10,000 searches and sky-high difficulty. Focus on keywords you can actually rank for.

Step 5: Prioritize Keywords by Business Value

You've now gathered a list of potential keywords with varying difficulty levels and search volumes. The final step in learning how to do keyword research is prioritizing which keywords to target first based on their potential business value.

Consider these factors when prioritizing:

  • Relevance to your offerings: Does this keyword directly relate to what you sell or offer?
  • Commercial intent: Are people searching this likely to become customers, or just casual browsers?
  • Ranking feasibility: Can you realistically rank for this within 3-6 months?
  • Search volume potential: Will enough people see your content if you rank?
  • Content creation effort: Do you have the resources and expertise to create excellent content for this topic?

Create a simple spreadsheet with your keywords and score each one on these factors. This helps you identify your "quick wins"—keywords with decent volume, low-to-moderate difficulty, and high commercial value.

For example, "custom wedding cakes Portland" might have lower search volume than "wedding cakes," but for a Portland bakery, it's far more valuable because it indicates strong local purchase intent and is much easier to rank for.

Building Your Content Strategy Around Keyword Research

Once you've completed your keyword research, the real work begins: creating content that targets these keywords and genuinely helps your audience. Your keyword list should inform your entire content roadmap, including:

  • Service and product pages: Target transactional keywords with clear commercial intent
  • Blog posts and guides: Target informational keywords that demonstrate your expertise and build trust
  • Local landing pages: Target geo-specific keywords if you serve particular locations
  • FAQ pages: Target question-based keywords that address common customer concerns

Remember, keyword research isn't a one-time activity. Search trends change, new competitors emerge, and your business evolves. Plan to revisit and refresh your keyword research every quarter to stay ahead of changes in your market.

The key to success is consistency. Create high-quality content around your prioritized keywords, optimize it properly, and give it time to gain traction in search results. Combined with other SEO best practices, thorough keyword research forms the foundation of sustainable organic traffic growth.

Common Keyword Research Mistakes to Avoid

Even when you know how to do keyword research, it's easy to fall into common traps that waste time and resources:

  • Targeting only high-volume keywords: These are almost always highly competitive and difficult to rank for as a small business
  • Ignoring long-tail keywords: Longer, more specific phrases often have lower competition and higher conversion rates
  • Forgetting about local intent: If you serve specific areas, including location modifiers is essential
  • Not considering seasonality: Some keywords spike at certain times of year—plan your content calendar accordingly
  • Keyword stuffing: Once you've identified your keywords, use them naturally in your content, not excessively
  • Creating content without checking existing rankings: If you're already ranking on page 2-3 for a keyword, optimizing that existing page may be smarter than creating new content

Avoid these pitfalls by staying focused on creating genuinely helpful content that serves your audience's needs, with keywords as a guide rather than an obsession.
