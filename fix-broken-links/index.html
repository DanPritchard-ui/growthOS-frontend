How to Find and Fix Broken Links on Your Website (Free Tools)

The complete technical guide to identifying, repairing, and preventing 404 errors that damage your SEO

By the RankActions teamAugust 5, 20268 min read
In this guide
  • How to find every broken link on your site — internal and outbound
  • Fix, redirect or remove: choosing the right repair for each link
  • Preventing new broken links as your site grows

Every website owner needs to fix broken links regularly to maintain strong search rankings and user experience. Broken links—also called dead links or 404 errors—damage your site's credibility with both users and search engines. When visitors click a link that leads nowhere, they often leave your site entirely. Meanwhile, search engines interpret broken links as signs of poor maintenance, which can hurt your rankings.

This comprehensive guide walks you through the entire process of identifying, repairing, and preventing broken links using free tools and proven techniques. Whether you manage a small blog or a large enterprise website, these strategies will help you maintain a healthy link profile.

Why You Need to Fix Broken Links

Broken links create multiple problems for your website. From an SEO perspective, they waste crawl budget—the limited resources search engines allocate to crawling your site. When Googlebot encounters numerous broken links, it spends time on pages that don't exist instead of discovering and indexing your valuable content.

User experience suffers dramatically when visitors encounter 404 errors. Research shows that users who hit broken links are 73% more likely to abandon a website immediately. This increases your bounce rate and decreases the likelihood of conversions, whether that means sales, sign-ups, or engagement.

Link equity is another casualty of broken links. When external sites link to pages that no longer exist, you lose the SEO value those backlinks would have provided. Internal broken links similarly waste the authority you've built within your own site structure.

Free Tools to Find Broken Links

Several excellent free tools can help you identify broken links across your website. Each offers different features suited to various needs and technical skill levels.

Google Search Console

Google Search Console remains the gold standard for identifying crawl errors. Navigate to the "Pages" report under "Indexing" to see which URLs return 404 errors. The tool shows you which pages link to the broken URLs, making it easier to locate and fix them. As an AI-powered SEO platform, we recommend checking Search Console weekly.

Screaming Frog SEO Spider

The free version of Screaming Frog crawls up to 500 URLs, making it ideal for small to medium websites. It identifies broken internal links, external links, images, and other resources. The spider provides detailed reports showing exactly where each broken link appears on your site.

Browser Extensions

Extensions like Check My Links for Chrome scan individual pages in seconds, highlighting broken links in red. These tools work well for quick spot-checks but aren't suitable for comprehensive site audits.

Pro Tip: Set up automated monitoring to catch broken links before they impact your rankings. Many modern SEO platforms offer real-time alerts when new 404 errors appear, allowing you to respond immediately rather than discovering problems weeks later during manual audits.

Step-by-Step Process to Fix Broken Links

Once you've identified broken links, follow this systematic approach to repair them efficiently.

Audit and Prioritize

Not all broken links demand immediate attention. Prioritize based on these criteria:

  • High-traffic pages: Fix broken links on your most visited pages first, as they affect the most users
  • Important internal links: Links from navigation menus, footers, and key landing pages take precedence
  • External backlinks: Broken pages that receive external links waste valuable SEO equity
  • Recent breaks: Newly broken links often indicate systemic issues that need immediate investigation

Choose Your Repair Method

You have several options when you fix broken links, depending on the situation:

Update the URL: If the destination page has moved, simply update the link to point to the new location. This is the cleanest solution when content still exists.

Implement 301 redirects: When you've permanently moved or renamed a page, set up 301 redirects from the old URL to the new one. This preserves link equity and ensures visitors reach the correct destination.

Remove the link: If the linked content no longer exists and has no suitable replacement, remove the hyperlink entirely. Keep the anchor text as plain text if it provides value to readers.

Replace with alternative content: Find a relevant replacement page and update the link to point there instead. Choose content that serves the same purpose for users.

Common Causes of Broken Links and How to Fix Broken Links Proactively

Understanding why links break helps you prevent future issues. Common causes include:

Website migrations: Moving to a new domain or restructuring your site often creates broken links. Always create comprehensive redirect maps before migrations and test thoroughly afterward.

Deleted or renamed pages: When you remove content or change URLs without implementing redirects, you create broken links. Make it standard practice to check for internal links before deleting any page.

External link rot: Websites you link to may remove content, change URLs, or go offline entirely. You can't control external sites, but you can monitor your outbound links regularly.

CMS or plugin issues: Content management system updates sometimes break links, especially custom permalinks or dynamically generated URLs. Test all major functionality after updates.

Preventive Maintenance Schedule

Implement a regular maintenance routine to catch broken links early:

  1. Run a full site crawl monthly using Screaming Frog or similar tools
  2. Check Google Search Console weekly for new crawl errors
  3. Review external backlink profiles quarterly to identify broken destination pages
  4. Test all internal navigation and footer links after any site updates
  5. Monitor high-traffic pages weekly for broken resources

Advanced Strategies to Fix Broken Links at Scale

Large websites with thousands of pages need more sophisticated approaches. Consider these advanced techniques:

Database-level audits: Query your database directly to identify orphaned content, missing redirects, and inconsistent URL patterns. This catches issues that surface-level crawls might miss.

Regex pattern matching: Use regular expressions to identify and bulk-update common link patterns. For example, if you changed your blog URL structure from "/blog/post-name" to "/articles/post-name", regex can update hundreds of links simultaneously.

Automated testing: Implement continuous integration tests that check for broken links before deploying changes to production. This prevents broken links from going live in the first place.

Link validation APIs: Build custom scripts that periodically test external links and flag dead destinations. This is especially valuable for resource pages and link directories.

Enterprise Tip: Large organizations should establish link governance policies. Document who can create links, approval workflows for structural changes, and mandatory testing protocols before major updates. This prevents the chaos that creates broken links at scale.

Measuring the Impact of Your Link Repair Work

Track these metrics to quantify the benefits of fixing broken links:

  • Crawl error reduction: Monitor the trend in Google Search Console's crawl errors report
  • Bounce rate improvements: Compare bounce rates on pages before and after fixing broken links
  • Indexation increases: Watch for more pages getting indexed as crawl budget is freed up
  • Internal link equity flow: Use log file analysis to verify that search engine bots can reach all important pages
  • User engagement metrics: Track time on site and pages per session as indicators of improved experience

Most websites see measurable improvements within 2-4 weeks of comprehensive link repair. The exact timeline depends on your site's size and how frequently search engines crawl it.

Final Thoughts on Maintaining a Healthy Link Profile

Learning to fix broken links is just one component of comprehensive technical SEO. It requires ongoing attention rather than one-time fixes. By implementing the tools, processes, and schedules outlined in this guide, you'll maintain a clean link profile that supports both user experience and search engine optimization.

Remember that prevention is more efficient than cure. Establish strong protocols before making structural changes, test thoroughly after updates, and monitor continuously for new issues. The investment in proper link maintenance pays dividends through improved rankings, better user engagement, and stronger site authority.
